Breast Size Chart — Visual Guide with Cup Sizes Comparison

A breast size chart is one of the most useful tools for finding bras that actually fit. Whether you are a first-time bra shopper, have been wearing the wrong size for years, or simply want a clearer visual understanding of cup sizes, this guide is for you.

This page provides a complete breast size chart with international conversions (US, UK, EU, JP), a sister size table, visual size descriptions, and a step-by-step measurement guide. Use it as a reference whenever you shop for bras, swimsuits, or any other fitted clothing.

Visual Breast Size Descriptions

Cup letters are abstract numbers — what does an "H cup" actually look like? Here are visual descriptions to help you understand what each cup range typically represents.

A Cup (1 inch difference)

A cup breasts are small but proportionate. They generally have a narrow root and a slight projection. A cup wearers often have more flexibility in bra styles and can wear bralettes, wireless, and lightly structured bras comfortably.

B Cup (2 inch difference)

B cup breasts are still small but with more visible projection. They are the second most common cup size in many countries. B cup wearers have a wide range of bra options including wireless, lightly structured, and most bralettes.

C Cup (3 inch difference)

C cup breasts are the most common cup size in many countries. They provide a balance of fullness and proportion that works for a wide range of body types. C cup wearers have nearly unlimited bra options.

D Cup (4 inch difference)

D cup breasts are considered moderately full. They typically require structured bras with adequate support, especially for high-impact activities. D cup wearers benefit from wide bands and side support.

DD Cup (5 inch difference)

DD cups (also called E cups in UK sizing) are fuller than average. They require structured bras with reinforced cups, wider bands, and side support. Most specialty bra brands start their extended sizing at DD.

DDD / F Cup (6 inch difference)

DDD cups (called F cups in EU and FF in UK) are full-bust sizes. They require bras engineered for support — full coverage, side support, wide bands, and strong straps. Many mainstream retailers cap their sizing at DDD.

G Cup (7 inch difference)

G cups (called F in UK, G in EU) are where specialty full-bust brands really shine. Bras in this size need to be specifically designed for fuller busts. The fit and engineering differences from DDD are significant.

H Cup (8 inch difference)

H cups (called FF in UK) are larger than average. They require full-coverage, side-support, or minimizer bras designed specifically for H+ cups. Brands like Elomi, Bravissimo, and Goddess specialize in this size range. See our complete H cup guide for more details.

I Cup and Above (9+ inch difference)

I cups and above (called G in UK, J in EU) are the largest commonly available sizes. Specialty full-bust brands like Elomi, Bravissimo, Panache, and Ewa Michalak are typically the only sources for these sizes.

How to Measure Your Breast Size: Step-by-Step

Follow these steps to measure your breast size at home. For best results, measure mid-cycle (between periods) when your breasts are at their average size.

Step 1: Measure Your Band Size

  1. Wear a non-padded bra or no bra.
  2. Wrap a soft measuring tape snugly around your ribcage, directly under your bust.
  3. Keep the tape level and parallel to the floor all the way around.
  4. Exhale normally and record the measurement in inches.
  5. If even, add 0 inches. If odd, add 1 inch. Round to the nearest even number for your band size.

Step 2: Measure Your Bust Size

  1. Wrap the measuring tape loosely around the fullest part of your bust, usually across the nipple line.
  2. Keep the tape level and do not compress the breast tissue.
  3. Record the measurement in inches.

Step 3: Calculate Your Cup Size

  1. Subtract your band size from your bust size.
  2. Use the difference to find your cup size on the chart above.

Step 4: Verify with Our Calculator

For a more accurate measurement that accounts for individual variations, use our Bra Size Calculator or visit a specialty bra boutique for a professional fitting.

Common Bra Sizing Mistakes

Most women are wearing the wrong bra size — research suggests 70–80% of bra wearers are in a size that does not fit properly. The most common mistakes include:

  • Wearing a band that is too loose. The band should be snug and level. If the band rides up in the back, it is too loose.
  • Wearing cups that are too small. Cups should fully contain the breast tissue with no spillage. If you see "side boob" or top spillage, the cups are too small.
  • Adjusting the straps too tight. The straps should be snug but not load-bearing. If the straps are doing most of the support work, the band is too loose.
  • Using the +4 method. The +4 method (adding 4 inches to the underbust measurement) is outdated and results in a band that is too loose and cups that are too small. Use the modern method described above for more accurate results.
  • Not adjusting as your body changes. Hormonal fluctuations, weight changes, pregnancy, and aging can all affect your bra size. Re-measure every 1–2 years, or any time you notice fit issues.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know my breast size?

To find your breast size, measure your band (snug underbust) and bust (across the fullest part of the breast) in inches. The difference between these two measurements gives your cup size — 1 inch = A cup, 2 inches = B cup, 3 inches = C cup, and so on. Use our bra size calculator for a more precise measurement that accounts for variations across sizing systems.

What is the average breast size?

The average breast size varies by country. In the United States, the average is around a 34DD (US) or 34E (UK). In the United Kingdom, the average is roughly a 36D. Globally, the average has increased over the last 20 years due to factors like nutrition, hormones, and changes in body composition.

How do US, UK, EU, and JP bra sizes differ?

Different countries use different sizing systems for bra cup letters. US, UK, and EU systems all use the same band sizing in inches (or centimeters in EU). However, cup letters differ: a US DDD is a UK E, an EU F, and a JP E. Always check the sizing chart for the specific brand you are buying from, as conversions can vary.

What are sister sizes?

Sister sizes are equivalent cup volumes on different band sizes. For example, a 36C has sister sizes 34D and 38B. If your usual band feels too tight, go up a band and down a cup; if too loose, go down a band and up a cup. This preserves the same cup volume while adjusting the band fit.

Why do my breast measurements change throughout the month?

Hormonal fluctuations during the menstrual cycle, pregnancy, and menopause can all cause breast size to vary. Many women experience a half to full cup size increase in the days before menstruation due to water retention and hormonal changes. This is normal and is why professional fitters recommend measuring breast size mid-cycle for the most accurate result.

What cup size should I aim for if I want a smaller appearance?

A minimizer bra is the best solution if you want to reduce the appearance of breast size without surgery. Minimizer bras redistribute breast tissue to create a smoother, more streamlined silhouette and can reduce apparent bust size by up to 2 inches. They are not designed to compress the breasts (which is uncomfortable and unhealthy) but to redistribute tissue across a wider area.
