How to Measure for Precise Volume Calculation

Accurate measurements are essential for reliable volume estimation. Use a soft measuring tape, stand upright, and measure in front of a mirror if possible.

Step 1 — Underbust & Bust

Measure your ribcage snugly just under the bust for underbust. Then measure around the fullest part of your bust, keeping the tape horizontal. These provide baseline data and are used in both Quick and Precise modes.

Step 2 — Base Width

Measure the horizontal width of your breast where it meets the chest wall (breast footprint). This typically ranges from 8-25 cm. A wider base correlates with larger overall volume.

Step 3 — Nipple to IMF

Measure the distance from the nipple straight down to the inframammary fold (the crease under your breast). This reflects vertical breast dimensions. Typical range: 4-15 cm.

Step 4 — Projection

Stand sideways to a mirror. Measure how far your breast projects outward from your chest wall at the fullest point. Typical range: 2-12 cm. Greater projection usually indicates higher volume.

For a complete measurement walkthrough, visit our guide on measuring bra size at home.

Frequently Asked Questions

What formula does the Precise mode use?

The Precise mode implements the BREAST-V formula modified for Asian female anthropometry: Volume = 15.6 × Base Width × Nipple-IMF × Projection − 142.5, with a 50 cc floor. This is then multiplied by shape correction (0.75-1.15) and ptosis correction (1.0-1.45) factors. The Quick mode uses a simplified geometric hemisphere model and is included for rapid screening only.

What is the typical breast volume range?

Breast volume varies widely across individuals. Average volumes by cup size: A cup ~150-250 cc, B ~250-350 cc, C ~350-500 cc, D ~500-700 cc, DD ~700-900 cc, F ~900-1100 cc per breast[1]. Asian women average ~340 cc per breast. These ranges depend on individual anatomy and body proportions.

What does “bra size calibration” do?

If you provide your known bra size (e.g. 75C), the calculator cross-references this against a standard Asian volume-to-cup mapping table. A calibration factor (clamped to 0.7-1.3x) is applied to pull the estimate toward real-world fit data. This helps compensate for individual anatomical variation that pure geometric formulas cannot capture.

How accurate is this tool?

In Precise mode: ±10-15% versus clinical MRI gold-standard measurements, based on validation against a cohort of 605 Asian patients[1]. In Quick mode: ±30-50%—use for informal reference only. Neither mode replaces professional medical imaging for clinical decisions.
