Breast Weight Calculator

Science-based breast weight estimation using the BREAST-V volumetric formula and tissue density (0.94 g/cm³). For bra fitting reference only.

⚠️ Medical Disclaimer: This calculator provides estimates for bra fitting reference only. It is not a medical device and cannot replace clinical measurement methods (MRI, 3D scanning, water displacement). Results involve manual measurement error and are not intended for medical diagnosis, treatment planning, or surgical decisions. Consult a healthcare professional for any medical concerns.

Measurement Guide for Weight Calculation

Use a soft measuring tape. Stand upright, breathe normally, and avoid compressing breast tissue. Measurements are external body surface values and subject to individual variation.

Important Measurement Notes

  • Natural stance: Stand upright with shoulders relaxed, arms at sides. Do not push your chest forward or hunch.
  • Normal breathing: Take measurements at the end of a normal exhale—do not hold your breath or take a deep breath.
  • Gentle contact: Rest the tape against the skin without pressing into or compressing the breast tissue.
  • Left-right differences: It is normal for left and right breasts to differ slightly in size. Measure each breast separately for best accuracy.
  • Mirror check: Use a mirror to ensure the tape is level and positioned correctly.

Step 1 — Breast Base Radius (R)

Measure the horizontal width of one breast where it meets the chest wall. Divide by 2 to get the radius. A larger base indicates wider breast footprint.

Step 2 — Projection Height (H)

Stand sideways to a mirror. Measure from the most forward point of the breast (nipple area) straight back to the chest wall plane. This is the projection height.

Step 3 — Advanced Mode (Optional)

For better accuracy, measure three dimensions: horizontal semi-width (a), anterior-posterior half-thickness (b), and vertical projection (c). This uses an ellipsoid model that better captures real breast shape.

For a complete measurement guide, see our guide on measuring bra size at home.

Frequently Asked Questions

What formula does this calculator use?

Simple Mode: V = (2/3) × π × R² × H (spherical cap approximation). Advanced Mode: V = (4/3) × π × a × b × c (modified ellipsoid model). Weight = Volume × Density × Population Coefficient. Breast tissue density is fixed at 0.94 g/cm³, a widely accepted value in plastic surgery and biomechanics research.

What do the symbols R, H, a, b, c mean?

R = Breast Base Radius — half the horizontal width of the breast at the chest wall. H = Projection Height — how far the breast projects forward from the chest wall. a = Horizontal Semi-Width — half the horizontal span of the breast. b = AP Half-Thickness — half the anterior-posterior depth. c = Vertical Projection — vertical height of the breast mound.

What is the typical weight of a breast?

Breast weight varies widely. A single breast typically weighs: A cup ~150–250g, B cup ~250–350g, C cup ~350–500g, D cup ~500–700g, DD+ ~700–1,000g+. These are approximate ranges based on average breast tissue density and volume.

How does breast weight affect bra selection?

Heavier breasts require more structural support: wider straps distribute pressure, reinforced underbands prevent sagging, full-coverage cups provide better weight distribution. Our calculator provides specific recommendations based on your calculated weight range.
