Frequently Asked Questions

How accurate is this shape classification?

The classification uses objective body measurements transformed into standardized shape dimensions, then matched against international bra industry classification vectors via cosine similarity. Accuracy depends on measurement precision—use a soft tape and follow each step's illustration carefully.

What do the 6 measurements mean?

Underbust: circumference directly under breasts, tight after exhale. Bust: circumference at fullest point, leaning forward 45°. Projection Height: distance from chest wall to nipple apex. Base Width: horizontal width of one breast at chest wall. Nipple Position: vertical offset from inframammary fold (negative = above). Spacing: gap between inner edges of both breasts.

Can my shape change over time?

Yes. Breast shape naturally changes with age, weight fluctuations, hormonal shifts (pregnancy, menopause), and fitness changes. Re-check your shape type every 6–12 months for optimal bra fit.

What does this breast shape mean?

Breast shape is independent of size. Two women with the same cup size can have very different shapes, which dramatically affects how bras fit. Knowing your shape helps you choose styles that work with your anatomy rather than against it.

  • Round / Hemispherical : Equal fullness top and bottom. Most bra styles work.
  • Teardrop / Pear : Slightly less full on top, fuller below. Balconette and plunge bras work well.
  • Shallow / Disc : Wide base, less projection. Push-up and demi-cup styles work well; full-cup often gaps.
  • Athletic / Wide-set : More tissue toward the sides. Side-support bras and racerback styles help.
  • Ptotic / Pendulous : Nipple points downward. Full-cup, side-support, and minimizer styles provide lift.
