Frequently Asked Questions

What is breast ptosis?

Breast ptosis, commonly known as breast sagging, is a natural condition where the breast tissue descends and the nipple falls below the inframammary fold. It is classified using the Regnault system: Grade I (mild, nipple at IMF level), Grade II (moderate, nipple below IMF but above lower breast contour), and Grade III (severe, nipple at lowest breast point).

Can bras prevent or reverse ptosis?

Wearing a well-fitted supportive bra can help prevent further stretching of Cooper's ligaments and reduce discomfort, but it cannot reverse existing ptosis. Research shows that consistent support during physical activity reduces strain on breast ligaments[1].

What causes breast ptosis?

Common factors include aging (loss of skin elasticity), pregnancy and breastfeeding, significant weight fluctuations, genetics, smoking, and inadequate breast support during exercise. Multiple pregnancies and larger breast size are associated with higher ptosis risk[2].

Clinical Reference: This tool uses a simplified version of the Regnault ptosis classification system. The actual clinical assessment involves multiple anatomical reference points[3]. For medical evaluation, please consult a healthcare professional.
