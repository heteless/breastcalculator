How to Measure Your Breast Size

Getting an accurate measurement is the first step to finding a bra that truly fits. Here’s a simple method you can do at home with just a soft measuring tape. No advanced tools needed.

Step 1 — Measure your underbust: Stand upright wearing a non-padded bra. Wrap the measuring tape around your ribcage, just under your bust. Make sure it’s snug but not too tight — that’s your band size (underbust measurement). Write down the number.

Step 2 — Measure your bust: Measure around the fullest part of your bust, keeping the tape parallel to the floor. Don’t pull it too tight — this is your bust measurement. The difference between your bust and underbust measurements determines your cup size.

Step 3 — Use our breast calculator: Enter your measurements into the breast calculator above to get your bra size across US, UK, EU, FR, and AU sizing systems instantly. For the most accurate results, measure first thing in the morning and avoid measuring during your menstrual period when breast tissue may be swollen.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is breast ptosis?

Breast ptosis, commonly known as breast sagging, is a natural condition where the breast tissue descends and the nipple falls below the inframammary fold. It is classified using the Regnault system: Grade I (mild, nipple at IMF level), Grade II (moderate, nipple below IMF but above lower breast contour), and Grade III (severe, nipple at lowest breast point).

Can bras prevent or reverse ptosis?

Wearing a well-fitted supportive bra can help prevent further stretching of Cooper's ligaments and reduce discomfort, but it cannot reverse existing ptosis. Research shows that consistent support during physical activity reduces strain on breast ligaments[1].

What causes breast ptosis?

Common factors include aging (loss of skin elasticity), pregnancy and breastfeeding, significant weight fluctuations, genetics, smoking, and inadequate breast support during exercise. Multiple pregnancies and larger breast size are associated with higher ptosis risk[2].
