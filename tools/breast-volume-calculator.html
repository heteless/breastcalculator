Frequently Asked Questions

How is breast volume calculated?

This tool uses a simplified hemispherical model: Volume is estimated based on the difference between bust and underbust circumference, assuming each breast occupies a portion of this difference. The formula is: Volume (per breast) = 0.5 × π × (radius of bust difference)² × (bust width/2). This provides a rough estimate and is not medically precise—actual breast volume varies with individual breast shape, density, and chest wall contour.

What is the typical breast volume range?

Breast volume varies widely. Average volumes by cup size: A cup ~150-250 cc, B ~250-350 cc, C ~350-500 cc, D ~500-700 cc, DD ~700-900 cc, F ~900-1100 cc per breast. These ranges depend on individual anatomy and body proportions. A study of Asian women found average breast volumes of 340 cc[1].

Is this useful for bra fitting?

Breast volume information can help you understand your size relative to standard bras. However, bra fitting depends more on band size, cup shape, and breast root width than volume alone. Use this as a general reference and rely on our bra size calculator for specific sizing.

Methodology: This calculator uses a geometric approximation. Clinical methods for measuring breast volume include MRI, 3D scanning, and the water displacement method[1]. This tool is for educational reference only.
