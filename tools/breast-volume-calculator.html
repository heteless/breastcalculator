How to Measure Your Breast Size

Getting an accurate measurement is the first step to finding a bra that truly fits. Here’s a simple method you can do at home with just a soft measuring tape. No advanced tools needed.

Step 1 — Measure your underbust: Stand upright wearing a non-padded bra. Wrap the measuring tape around your ribcage, just under your bust. Make sure it’s snug but not too tight — that’s your band size (underbust measurement). Write down the number.

Step 2 — Measure your bust: Measure around the fullest part of your bust, keeping the tape parallel to the floor. Don’t pull it too tight — this is your bust measurement. The difference between your bust and underbust measurements determines your cup size.

Step 3 — Use our breast calculator: Enter your measurements into the breast calculator above to get your bra size across US, UK, EU, FR, and AU sizing systems instantly. For the most accurate results, measure first thing in the morning and avoid measuring during your menstrual period when breast tissue may be swollen.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is breast volume calculated?

This tool uses a simplified hemispherical model: Volume is estimated based on the difference between bust and underbust circumference, assuming each breast occupies a portion of this difference. The formula is: Volume (per breast) = 0.5 × π × (radius of bust difference)² × (bust width/2). This provides a rough estimate and is not medically precise—actual breast volume varies with individual breast shape, density, and chest wall contour.

What is the typical breast volume range?

Breast volume varies widely. Average volumes by cup size: A cup ~150-250 cc, B ~250-350 cc, C ~350-500 cc, D ~500-700 cc, DD ~700-900 cc, F ~900-1100 cc per breast. These ranges depend on individual anatomy and body proportions. A study of Asian women found average breast volumes of 340 cc[1].

Is this useful for bra fitting?

Breast volume information can help you understand your size relative to standard bras. However, bra fitting depends more on band size, cup shape, and breast root width than volume alone. Use this as a general reference and rely on our breast calculator for specific sizing.
