Breast Expansion (Splaying) — Causes and Corrective Support

Understanding breast splaying from an anatomical perspective, and how bra design can provide corrective support for a more centered bust profile.

What Is Breast Splaying?

Breast splaying, also referred to as lateral breast displacement or wide-set breasts, describes a condition where the breast tissue is distributed toward the sides of the chest, resulting in a wider intermammary distance (the space between the breasts) and nipples that point outward rather than forward[1]. It is important to note that splaying is a natural anatomical variation, not a disease.

Anatomical and Genetic Factors

Breast Base Width

The width of the breast base attachment to the chest wall varies considerably among women. Some women naturally have a broader breast base, which positions the nipples further apart[2]. This is largely genetically determined and is not a sign of any abnormality.

Cooper's Ligament Laxity

As with ptosis, the integrity of Cooper's ligaments plays a role in splaying. When these ligaments weaken with age, repeated weight fluctuations, or insufficient support, the breast tissue may gradually migrate laterally, increasing the appearance of splaying[3].

Clinical Perspective on Splaying

Splaying is primarily an aesthetic concern for most women. However, it can affect bra fit in several ways: wider-set breasts may experience gore (center panel) lift-off, where the bra's center fails to lie flat against the sternum. This reduces the overall effectiveness of the bra's support system, particularly during physical activity.

Bra Design Solutions for Splayed Breasts

Center-Pull Design

Bras designed for splayed breasts often incorporate a center-pull design, where the straps attach closer to the center gore rather than at the outer edges of the cups. This pulls the breasts inward toward the center of the chest, creating a more forward-facing breast profile[4].

Plunge and Balconette Styles

Plunge bras and balconette bras typically have lower gores, making them easier to fit on women with wider-set breasts. The lower gore is less likely to lift off the sternum, allowing for better band contact and more secure support.

Side Support Panels

Many bras designed for full-busted women include side support panels (also called side slings or power bars) that extend from the side of the cup toward the center. These panels provide lateral stability and help redirect breast tissue forward, counteracting lateral migration[4].
