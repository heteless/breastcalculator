Post-Mastectomy Care Guide — Recovery, Scar Care, and Finding Comfort

By Dr Cryptomeria, PhD in Fashion Design · May 22, 2026 · 7 min read

Recovery after mastectomy is a journey that affects your body, mind, and spirit. Proper post-operative care is essential for healing, regaining strength, and adjusting to life after breast cancer surgery. This comprehensive guide covers the physical and emotional aspects of recovery, including wound care, bra selection, exercises, and long-term wellness.

Important: This article provides general educational information. Always follow your surgical team's specific instructions for your recovery.

Post-Mastectomy Recovery Timeline

Recovery from mastectomy varies for each individual, but here is a general timeline of what to expect:

Week 1-2: Immediate Recovery

  • You will likely stay in the hospital for 1-3 days after surgery
  • Drainage tubes may be in place to remove excess fluid
  • Pain medication will be provided to manage discomfort
  • Minimal arm movement on the surgical side is recommended
  • Wear the post-surgical bra provided by your hospital
  • Sleep on your back with pillows supporting your arms

Week 3-6: Early Recovery

  • Drainage tubes are typically removed within 1-2 weeks
  • Gentle range-of-motion exercises can begin (with approval)
  • Continue wearing a soft, front-closure bra without underwire
  • Scar tissue begins to form around the incision
  • Swelling and bruising will start to subside
  • Most women can return to desk work during this period

Week 7-12: Progressive Recovery

  • Incision should be well-healed
  • Can transition to a prosthetic bra with breast forms (if desired)
  • Gradually increase arm and shoulder exercises
  • Scar massage can begin (with surgeon's approval)
  • Discuss reconstruction options with your surgical team

3-6 Months: Long-Term Recovery

  • Most daily activities can be resumed
  • Consider lymphedema prevention strategies
  • Build strength gradually through appropriate exercise
  • Regular follow-up appointments with your care team

Choosing the Right Bra After Mastectomy

Your bra needs will change throughout your recovery. Here is a guide to what to wear at each stage:

Immediate Post-Surgery (Weeks 0-6)

  • Post-Surgical Bra: Soft, front-closure bra provided by your hospital. Designed to hold dressings in place and minimize movement.
  • Key Features: Front closure, no underwire, soft cotton fabric, adjustable straps, stretchy material to accommodate drainage tubes.
  • Tips: You may need 2-3 bras to rotate while washing. Hand wash in gentle detergent.

Transition Period (Weeks 6-12)

  • Soft Pocketed Bra: Transition to a bra with light pockets that can hold a foam or light silicone breast form.
  • Comfort-First Approach: Focus on bras that do not put pressure on incisions or scars.
  • Professional Fitting: Consider working with a certified mastectomy fitter to find the right size and style.

Long-Term Daily Wear (After 12 Weeks)

  • Prosthetic Bras: Bras with secure pockets for silicone breast forms. Choose between front-closure and back-closure styles.
  • Regular Bras (with adhesive prostheses): Some women prefer adhesive breast forms that allow them to wear regular bras.
  • Activewear: Sports bras and swimwear designed for post-mastectomy use with built-in pockets.

For more detailed information about prosthetic bras, see our Prosthetic Bras Guide.

Scar Care and Incision Management

Proper scar care can improve healing and reduce scar visibility over time:

  • Keep the area clean and dry: Follow your surgeon's instructions for wound care and showering
  • Scar massage: Once healed (usually after 6-8 weeks), gentle massage can help soften scar tissue and improve mobility
  • Silicone sheets or gels: These can help flatten and lighten scars over time
  • Sun protection: Protect surgical scars from direct sunlight for at least 12 months
  • Moisturize: Keep the skin around your incisions moisturized with gentle, fragrance-free products

Exercises After Mastectomy

Gentle exercises are important for regaining range of motion and preventing stiffness. Always get approval from your surgeon before starting any exercise program:

  1. Deep Breathing (Days 1-7): Take slow, deep breaths to help your lungs expand fully after anesthesia
  2. Pendulum Exercises (Week 2-4): Gently lean forward and let your arm swing like a pendulum to improve shoulder mobility
  3. Wall Climbing (Week 4-8): Use your fingers to "walk up" a wall to gradually increase arm reach
  4. Shoulder Rolls (Week 4-8): Gentle shoulder rolls forward and backward to release tension
  5. Stretching (Week 8+): Gradually increase stretching under the guidance of a physical therapist

Lymphedema Awareness and Prevention

Lymphedema is swelling that can occur after lymph node removal during mastectomy. Approximately 20-30% of breast cancer survivors develop lymphedema. Prevention strategies include:

  • Avoid blood pressure measurements on the surgical side (inform all healthcare providers)
  • Avoid heavy lifting with the affected arm
  • Wear compression sleeves during air travel if recommended
  • Keep skin clean and moisturized to prevent infections
  • Watch for early signs: swelling, heaviness, tightness in the arm
  • Consult a lymphedema specialist for personalized prevention

Emotional Well-Being After Mastectomy

Emotional recovery is just as important as physical healing. Many women experience a range of emotions after mastectomy:

  • Give yourself time: It is normal to feel grief, anger, sadness, or anxiety after losing a breast
  • Seek support: Support groups, counseling, and talking with other breast cancer survivors can help
  • Focus on what your body can do: Celebrate milestones in your recovery and strength
  • Consider reconstruction timing: Some women choose immediate reconstruction; others wait months or years
  • Explore breast forms and prosthetics: Many women find that wearing a well-fitted prosthesis helps them feel more confident

Frequently Asked Questions

How long does it take to fully recover from mastectomy?

Most women feel significantly better within 4-6 weeks. Full recovery, including regaining range of motion and adjusting to physical changes, can take 3-6 months or longer. Every person's recovery is different.

When can I wear a regular bra after mastectomy?

Most surgeons recommend waiting at least 6-8 weeks before wearing any bra with underwire. Even after healing, many women find that soft, wire-free prosthetic bras are more comfortable than regular bras.

Can I exercise after mastectomy?

Yes, but start slowly and get approval from your surgeon. Begin with gentle stretching and range-of-motion exercises, then gradually increase intensity. Avoid heavy lifting with the affected arm for at least 4-6 weeks.

Will I need physical therapy after mastectomy?

Many women benefit from physical therapy to regain range of motion, prevent lymphedema, and rebuild strength. Ask your surgeon for a referral to a physical therapist experienced in post-mastectomy rehabilitation.

How do I manage pain after mastectomy?

Post-surgical pain is typically managed with prescribed medication. After the initial recovery period, some women experience chronic pain or neuropathy. Discuss any ongoing pain with your medical team.

Are there support groups for post-mastectomy recovery?

Yes. Organizations like the American Cancer Society, Breastcancer.org, and local hospitals offer support groups for breast cancer survivors. Online communities can also provide valuable peer support.
