US vs UK Bra Sizes: What's the Difference?

Shopping for a bra online usually means comparing size charts across countries. Two labels in the same drawer may look almost identical — a 34DD from a US brand and a 34E from a UK brand — but they are actually the same cup. The difference is just the way the alphabet progresses past D.

Our bra size calculator outputs US, UK, EU, FR, and AU side by side so you can shop any brand with confidence. This guide explains where the two systems diverge, why it happens, and how to read a conversion chart.

Why Do US and UK Cup Letters Differ?

Both systems start with the same idea: each additional cup letter represents one inch of difference between your underbust and your bust. So 1" = A , 2" = B , 3" = C , 4" = D in both countries.

Past D, the systems split. The US system simply adds new letters in alphabetical order: D, DD, DDD, G, H, I… The UK system uses a more compact progression: D, DD, E, F, FF, G, GG… This means:

  • US DDD = UK E
  • US G = UK F
  • US I = UK G

The cup volume is identical. Only the label changes.

Pattern to remember: past US DDD, the UK size is one letter after the US one — DDD → E, G → F (UK is one behind), I → G, J → H, and so on.

Why the Difference Exists

Two reasons explain the split.

1. Marketing perception. Many shoppers in the US associate double letters (DD, DDD) with a more modest cup progression. The UK system chose E, F, FF, G because it compresses the alphabet and reads as a more sequential "ladder" of cup sizes.

2. Historical precedent. UK brands (Panache, Freya, Elomi, Curvy Kate) historically led the fuller-cup market. They standardized on E, F, FF early on. US brands (ThirdLove, Victoria's Secret) standardized on DDD, G, H. Both are correct; they just count differently.

EU, FR, and AU Sizing: A Quick Note

EU and French (FR) sizes use the same system: the band is measured in centimeters (rounded to multiples of 5), and the cup progression is similar to the UK system (A, B, C, D, E, F…) but starting from a different base. Australian (AU) sizing follows the UK system exactly.

The easiest way to convert between any of these systems is to use the bra size calculator , which gives you all four side by side from a single measurement.

How to Convert Your Size in 3 Steps

  1. Measure once. Take your underbust (snug, in inches) and your bust (loose, in inches) with a soft measuring tape.
  2. Enter into the calculator. Open the bra size calculator at the top of the home page and enter both numbers.
  3. Read all four systems. The output shows your US, UK, EU, and FR sizes on a single line. Save the line as a note on your phone so you can reference it when shopping any brand.

Common Pitfalls When Shopping Across Systems

1. "US 36D = UK 36D" is wrong above D. Always check the conversion chart above or use the calculator. A 36DDD in the US is a 36E in the UK — not a 36DDD.

2. Band sizes are not always equal. A US 36 and a UK 36 are the same length, but some EU brands measure band length rather than underbust circumference, which can make the same labeled size fit very differently. If a 36 in one brand feels too tight, try a sister size (see below).

3. "DD" doesn't always mean what you think. In both systems, DD = roughly 5" of difference between band and bust. That's a moderate cup, not an extreme one. Many people wear DD and don't realize it.

4. Sizing changes by brand, not just by country. A 34DD in brand A and a 34DD in brand B can fit differently because cup shape and band stretch vary. Always check the brand's own size guide and reviews before buying.

Sister Sizes: Your Cross-System Backup Plan

When your exact size is out of stock, sister sizes let you keep the same cup volume on a different band. For example, 34DD, 32E (UK), and 36D all have the same cup volume. Sister sizing is a band-up-or-down adjustment that doesn't require a new calculator — just memorize the rule:

  • Band up, cup down: 34DD → 36D
  • Band down, cup up: 34DD → 32E (UK) or 32DDD (US)

This is the same rule in both systems — only the cup letters shift. See the complete sister sizes guide for charts from 28A to 44H.

Summary

US and UK cup letters diverge after D. Up to D, both systems are identical. Past D, the UK is always one letter behind the US (US DDD = UK E, US G = UK F, and so on). The cup volume is the same. Use the bra size calculator to convert any measurement to all four systems at once, and keep the sister size rule in your back pocket for out-of-stock situations.
