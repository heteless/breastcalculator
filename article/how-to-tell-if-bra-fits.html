How to Tell If Your Bra Fits Correctly — 5 Signs to Check

Reviewed by our editorial team · Updated May 2026 · 5 min read

Wearing the wrong bra size is surprisingly common — studies suggest up to 80% of women wear a bra that doesn't fit properly. A poorly fitted bra can cause shoulder pain, back discomfort, skin irritation, and even affect your posture over time.

Knowing how to tell if your bra fits correctly is essential for your comfort and breast health. Use this 5-point checklist to evaluate your bra fit at home, or use our free bra size calculator to find your accurate size first.

Sign 1: Check the Band Position

Your bra band provides approximately 80% of your bra's support, making it the most critical component of a good fit. A properly fitted band should sit horizontally around your ribcage, parallel to the floor.

  • Too loose: The band rides up your back when you raise your arms
  • Too tight: The band digs into your skin, leaving red marks
  • Correct fit: The band stays level all the way around, and you can fit two fingers comfortably under it

If your band rides up, try going down one band size. If it digs in, try going up one band size and adjust your cup size accordingly using sister sizing.

Sign 2: Examine the Cup Coverage

The cups of your bra should fully contain your breast tissue without any spillage or gaps.

  • Spilling over the top: Your cup size is too small — try going up one cup size
  • Gapping at the top of the cup: Your cup size is too large, or you may need a different cup shape (e.g., balconette vs. full coverage)
  • Spilling at the sides (armpit area): The cup is too narrow, or you need a style with wider side coverage
  • Correct fit: Your breast tissue sits smoothly inside the cups with no overflow or empty space

Sign 3: Assess the Center Gore

The center gore is the piece of fabric between the two cups. In a properly fitted bra, it should lie flat against your breastbone (sternum).

  • Floating away from your chest: The cups are too small, or the band is too large — try a smaller band or larger cup
  • Digging in: The gore is too wide for your chest — try a different bra style
  • Correct fit: The gore sits flush against your sternum without pressing uncomfortably

Sign 4: Check the Shoulder Straps

While shoulder straps help with fit, they should not be doing the heavy lifting. If your straps are digging in or slipping off, it indicates a band size issue.

  • Straps dig in: The band may be too loose, causing the straps to bear too much weight
  • Straps slip off: The band may be too large, or you may need a bra with a racerback or convertible strap design
  • Correct fit: Straps stay in place without digging in, and you can slip one finger under them comfortably

Sign 5: Evaluate Overall Comfort

Ultimately, a well-fitted bra should feel comfortable. You should be able to wear it for a full day without discomfort.

  • No red marks or indentations on your shoulders or ribs
  • No breast tissue spilling out anywhere
  • You can breathe and move freely
  • The bra stays in place throughout the day without needing adjustment
  • No pain or discomfort in your back, neck, or shoulders

What to Do If Your Bra Doesn't Fit

If you've checked all five signs and your bra doesn't fit properly, don't worry — most women are in the same boat. Here's what to do:

  1. Use our bra size calculator to get your accurate measurements
  2. Try your sister sizes — bras with the same cup volume but different band and cup combinations
  3. Consider different bra styles — balconette, full coverage, plunge, and sports bras all fit differently
  4. Get fitted professionally if possible, or use our breast ptosis test and splaying assessment for additional guidance

A properly fitted bra can transform your comfort, confidence, and even your silhouette. Take a few minutes to check your bra fit today — your body will thank you.
