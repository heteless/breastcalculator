How to Measure Bra Size at Home — Complete Step-by-Step Guide

By Dr Cryptomeria, PhD in Fashion Design · May 5, 2026 · 7 min read

Knowing how to measure your bra size at home is the first step toward finding a bra that fits comfortably and provides proper support. Studies show that up to 80% of women wear the wrong bra size, which can lead to back pain, shoulder discomfort, and poor posture.

This complete guide walks you through the bra measurement process step by step. Whether you need your US, UK, EU, or international bra size, the process starts the same way — with accurate band and bust measurements.

What You Need to Measure Bra Size

  • A soft measuring tape (tailor's tape)
  • A non-padded bra (for most accurate bust measurement)
  • A mirror to check tape position
  • Paper and pen to record your numbers

Step 1: Measure Your Band Size (Underbust)

Your band size is the foundation of your bra fit. A properly fitted band provides about 80% of your bra's support.

  1. Wrap the measuring tape around your ribcage, directly under your bust
  2. Ensure the tape is parallel to the floor and snug against your skin
  3. Take a deep breath and note the measurement on the exhale
  4. Round to the nearest whole inch
  5. If the number is odd, add 1 inch to get your band size; if even, that's your band size

Step 2: Measure Your Bust Size

Your bust measurement helps determine your cup size. This should be taken at the fullest part of your bust.

  1. Stand straight with your arms at your sides
  2. Wrap the tape around the fullest part of your bust
  3. Keep the tape parallel to the floor — check in a mirror
  4. The tape should be snug but not tight enough to compress breast tissue
  5. Record the measurement to the nearest whole inch

Step 3: Calculate Your Cup Size

Your cup size is determined by the difference between your bust and band measurements.

Cup Size Formula: Bust measurement - Band measurement = Difference in inches

  • Less than 1 inch = AA cup
  • 1 inch difference = A cup
  • 2 inches = B cup
  • 3 inches = C cup
  • 4 inches = D cup
  • 5 inches = DD cup
  • 6 inches = DDD (or E in UK sizing)
  • 7 inches = G cup (or F in UK sizing)

Understanding Sister Sizes

Sister sizes are bras that have the same cup volume but different band and cup letter combinations. For example, if you wear a 34C, your sister sizes would be 32D and 36B. This is helpful when your exact size is not available or when you're between sizes.

How sister sizing works: Go up one band size and down one cup letter, or down one band size and up one cup letter, to maintain the same cup volume.

Common Bra Measurement Mistakes

  • Measuring over clothing: Always measure directly against your skin for accurate results
  • Tape too loose or too tight: The tape should be snug enough to stay in place but not leave marks
  • Wrong tape position: Ensure the tape stays parallel to the floor at all times
  • Using a padded bra: Padding adds volume and leads to an inaccurate bust measurement

When to Remeasure Your Bra Size

Your bra size can change throughout your life. It's recommended to remeasure:

  • After significant weight changes (gain or loss of 10+ pounds)
  • After pregnancy and breastfeeding
  • After starting or stopping hormonal birth control
  • Every 6-12 months as part of regular bra fitting maintenance
