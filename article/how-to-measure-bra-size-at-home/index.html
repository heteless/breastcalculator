How to Measure Bra Size at Home — Complete Step-by-Step Guide

Accurate at-home bra measurement in 3 simple steps. Use a soft tape measure to find your true band and cup size in just 5 minutes.

Why Accurate Measurement Matters

An estimated 70–80% of women wear the wrong bra size[1], often settling for a band that is too large and a cup that is too small. The result: discomfort, poor support, and visible fit problems. The good news is that you can find your true size in about five minutes with just a soft measuring tape.

This guide walks you through the same method used by professional bra fitters. For an instant digital estimate, you can also try our Bra Size Calculator after measuring.

What You Need

  • A soft, flexible measuring tape (cloth or vinyl, not rigid)
  • A mirror (helpful but not required)
  • A well-fitting, non-padded bra or no bra at all
  • A pen and the measurement log (optional)

Step 1 — Measure Your Underbust (Band Size)

Stand upright with relaxed shoulders. Wrap the measuring tape snugly around your ribcage directly under the breast fold, keeping the tape horizontal and parallel to the floor. Exhale normally and take the reading.

The two-finger test: You should be able to slide two fingers under the tape without straining. If you cannot, the tape is too tight; if you can fit more than two fingers comfortably, the tape is too loose.

Step 2 — Measure Your Bust (Cup Size)

Still standing upright, wrap the measuring tape around the fullest part of your bust — typically across the nipple line. Keep the tape horizontal, level, and not too tight. The tape should rest on the skin without compressing breast tissue.

Step 3 — Combine Band and Cup

For example, a 34-inch underbust and a 37-inch bust gives 34D (3-inch difference = D cup).

Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • Measuring over clothing: Adds bulk and gives an inaccurate reading. Measure directly on the skin or over an unpadded bra.
  • Pulling the tape too tight: A tight tape compresses tissue and under-reports cup volume.
  • Letting the tape sag in the back: Keep it level. A sagging tape inflates the bust measurement.
  • Measuring after a large meal or during menstruation: Hormonal water retention can change measurements by up to a full cup size[2].

What to Do Next

After taking your measurements, use the Bra Size Calculator to convert to US, UK, EU, FR, and AU sizing systems. Then check out our Bra Fit Checklist to verify that your new bra actually fits, and our Sister Sizes guide to find alternative fits when your exact size is out of stock.

FAQs

Should I round up or down for an odd underbust?

Round up to the next even number. So an underbust of 29 inches is a 30 band, and 31 inches is a 32 band. This matches the way most bras are manufactured (in 2-inch band increments).

Do I measure differently for sports bras?

The underbust measurement is identical. For the bust, lean forward 45 degrees so gravity pulls the tissue forward, then measure. Sports bras are typically worn snugger, so many women size down in the band for high-impact support.

How often should I re-measure?

Re-measure every 6–12 months, after any significant weight change (> 5 kg / 11 lb), or after pregnancy. Hormonal shifts during perimenopause can change breast size and shape noticeably over 12–24 months[3].
