Prosthetic Bras Guide — Finding Comfort and Confidence After Mastectomy

How to choose a comfortable, supportive bra after mastectomy. Pocketed cup styles, prosthesis options, post-surgery comfort, and where to find specialty fitters.

What Is a Prosthetic Bra?

A prosthetic bra (also called a mastectomy bra or post-surgery bra) is a bra designed with built-in pockets inside each cup to hold a breast prosthesis (a silicone or foam breast form). These bras restore symmetry, balance, and confidence after single or double mastectomy. They look and fit like regular bras from the outside.

When to Start Wearing a Prosthetic Bra

Most surgeons recommend waiting 6–8 weeks after mastectomy before being fitted for a permanent prosthetic bra. During the immediate recovery period, a soft, post-surgical compression bra (without underwire and with front closure) is usually recommended. Once the surgical site has healed and any drains are removed, you can transition to a fitted prosthetic bra.

Types of Breast Prostheses

Silicone Prostheses

Medical-grade silicone prostheses match the weight, drape, and feel of natural breast tissue. They come in a range of shapes (triangular, teardrop, oval) and sizes, and some are designed for specific surgeries (e.g., shallow forms for skin-sparing mastectomy). Silicone prostheses are typically worn 6–8 weeks after surgery.

Foam or Fiberfill Prostheses

Lightweight prostheses made from foam, fiberfill, or microfiber. These are often used immediately after surgery, during exercise, or in warmer climates where silicone can feel heavy or hot. They are not as realistic in feel but are comfortable and easy to care for.

Partial Prostheses (Shapers)

Designed for lumpectomy, reconstruction asymmetry, or natural asymmetry. A partial prosthesis fits inside a regular bra pocket to balance the appearance without a full-form.

Swim Prostheses

Lightweight, water-resistant forms designed for use in swimming pools and the ocean. They do not absorb water and resist chlorine and salt damage.

Choosing the Right Mastectomy Bra

Key Features to Look For

  • Pocketed cups: Soft cotton or modal lining with a discreet pocket for the prosthesis
  • Wide, cushioned straps: Distribute weight comfortably, especially important for larger prostheses
  • Soft, seamless fabric: Reduces irritation over sensitive scar tissue
  • Adjustable band: Allows for weight fluctuations and changes in chest wall contour
  • Front or back closure: Front-closure styles are easier during the first weeks of recovery
  • No underwire (initially): Wire-free styles are recommended in the first 6–12 months after surgery

Fitting Tips

Always be fitted by a certified mastectomy fitter (usually available at specialty lingerie shops, hospital breast centers, or via your oncology team). Bring your prosthesis to the fitting so the bra pocket can be matched to the form's dimensions. Expect to be re-fitted every 1–2 years or after any significant weight change.

Insurance Coverage

In the United States, the Women's Health and Cancer Rights Act (WHCRA) of 1998 requires most insurance plans that cover mastectomy to also cover breast prostheses and mastectomy bras. Most plans cover one new prosthesis every 1–2 years and 4–6 mastectomy bras per year. Ask your surgeon's office for a prescription and a list of in-network durable medical equipment (DME) suppliers.

Where to Find a Certified Fitter

  • Hospital breast centers and oncology departments
  • Specialty lingerie boutiques (Bravissimo, AnaOno, Nordstrom mastectomy departments)
  • American Cancer Society "tlc" program
  • National Lymphedema Network referrals

FAQs

How long does a silicone prosthesis last?

With daily wear and proper care, a high-quality silicone prosthesis lasts 2–3 years. Replace if you notice any change in shape, leaks, or the outer shell becomes sticky (a sign of degradation).

Can I sleep in a prosthetic bra?

Yes. A soft, wire-free mastectomy bra is often more comfortable than going braless, especially for larger prostheses. Some women prefer a lightweight leisure bra for sleep.

Is there a prosthetic for swimming?

Yes. Swim forms are made of waterproof silicone or foam and are designed to maintain their shape in chlorinated and salt water. They are also slightly lighter than everyday prostheses.
