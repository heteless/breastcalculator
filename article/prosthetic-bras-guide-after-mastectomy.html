Prosthetic Bras Guide — Finding Comfort and Confidence After Mastectomy

By Dr Cryptomeria, PhD in Fashion Design · May 25, 2026 · 6 min read

After a mastectomy or lumpectomy, finding the right bra is about more than just clothing — it is about comfort, confidence, and healing. Prosthetic bras (also called post-mastectomy bras or mastectomy bras) are specially designed to hold breast forms securely and comfortably, helping you feel balanced and natural after surgery.

This guide covers everything you need to know about prosthetic bras, how to choose the right one, and what to expect during your recovery journey.

What Is a Prosthetic Bra?

A prosthetic bra is a specially designed bra with built-in pockets to hold a breast prosthesis (breast form). These bras provide:

  • Secure pockets to hold silicone or foam breast forms
  • Soft, non-irritating fabric that is gentle on sensitive skin and scars
  • Wider straps and bands for additional comfort and support
  • Front closure options for easier dressing during recovery
  • A natural shape that restores breast symmetry and balance

Types of Post-Mastectomy Bras

There are several types of bras designed for different stages of recovery and daily wear:

1. Post-Surgical Bras (Immediate Recovery)

These are worn immediately after surgery, typically for the first 4-6 weeks. Features include:

  • Front closure for easy dressing
  • Soft, stretchy fabric that accommodates drainage tubes
  • No underwire to avoid pressure on surgical sites
  • Adjustable straps to accommodate swelling changes

2. Prosthetic Bras with Pockets

Once healed (typically after 6-8 weeks), you can transition to bras with built-in pockets for breast forms:

  • Designed to hold silicone or foam breast prostheses
  • Full-coverage cups for a natural silhouette
  • Wide, comfortable straps that do not dig into shoulders
  • Available in front-closure and back-closure styles

3. Mastectomy Swimwear and Activewear

For exercise and swimming, specialized options include:

  • Pocketed swimsuits with built-in bra support
  • Water-resistant breast forms designed for swimming
  • High-support sports bras with prosthesis pockets

Understanding Breast Prostheses (Breast Forms)

Breast prostheses are artificial forms that replace the natural breast tissue. They come in various types:

  • Silicone Breast Forms: Most popular option. Weighs similarly to natural breast tissue, feels realistic, and maintains body heat. Available in various shapes and sizes to match your remaining breast.
  • Foam Breast Forms: Lightweight option, ideal for immediate post-surgery use or for active wear. Less weight on the chest wall.
  • Partial Prostheses: For women who have had a lumpectomy or partial mastectomy. These fill in only the area where tissue was removed.
  • Adhesive Prostheses: Attach directly to the chest wall, allowing you to wear non-pocketed bras or clothing.

How to Choose the Right Prosthetic Bra

Choosing the right prosthetic bra involves several considerations:

  1. Stage of Recovery: Start with post-surgical bras, then transition to pocketed prosthetic bras once healed.
  2. Type of Surgery: Unilateral (one breast) vs. bilateral (both breasts) mastectomy affects your choices.
  3. Body Shape and Lifestyle: Consider your daily activities, exercise habits, and clothing preferences.
  4. Professional Fitting: Work with a certified mastectomy fitter who can measure you accurately and help you choose the right bra and prosthesis.
  5. Insurance Coverage: Many insurance plans cover mastectomy bras and prostheses. Check with your provider about coverage and requirements.

Tips for Measuring for a Prosthetic Bra

Getting accurate measurements is just as important for prosthetic bras as for regular bras. Here are specific tips:

  • Wear your breast form (prosthesis) during measurement if you have one
  • Measure the band size snugly around the ribcage
  • The band should feel firm but comfortable — it provides most of the support
  • Measure at the fullest part of the remaining breast (for unilateral mastectomy)
  • Consult with a mastectomy fitter for the most accurate sizing

Caring for Your Prosthetic Bra and Breast Form

  • Hand wash bras in mild detergent and air dry
  • Clean silicone breast forms with mild soap and water
  • Store breast forms flat in a cool, dry place
  • Replace bras every 6-12 months or when elasticity diminishes
  • Replace silicone prostheses every 1-2 years as they may change shape over time

Frequently Asked Questions

When can I start wearing a prosthetic bra after surgery?

Most surgeons recommend waiting 4-6 weeks after surgery before wearing a bra with a breast form. During the initial recovery period, a soft post-surgical bra is typically used. Always follow your surgeon's specific recommendations.

Can I wear a regular bra after mastectomy?

Yes, some women choose to wear regular bras with adhesive breast forms or after reconstruction. However, prosthetic bras offer better comfort, pocket security, and support for breast forms.

Are prosthetic bras covered by insurance?

In many countries, including the United States, mastectomy bras and breast prostheses are covered by insurance. The Women's Health and Cancer Rights Act (WHCRA) requires group health plans to cover breast reconstruction and prostheses after mastectomy.

How do I know what size breast form I need?

A certified mastectomy fitter can help you determine the right size and shape of breast form. The goal is to match the weight, size, and shape of your remaining breast for natural symmetry.
